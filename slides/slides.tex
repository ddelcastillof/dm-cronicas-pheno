\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Introduction (i)}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Cardiovascular disease is a global health threat, projected to become one of the largest contributors to disease burden by 2100, particularly in LMICs.
    \item Type 2 diabetes (T2D) is one of its major contributors, while type 1 diabetes (T1D) classically was not. 
    \item However, diabetes is heterogeneous. Insulin resistance and autoimmunity overlap on a spectrum, and many individuals show features of both.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Introduction (ii)}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A data-driven framework from clinical parameters has been previously proposed in individuals from European ancestry, with different risk of micro and macrovascular complications.
    \item A more informative categorization of diabetic patients will provide insigths on patient CVD stratification in resource-constrained settings.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
%=====================================
% Methods
%=====================================
\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Specific Aims}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item To classify individuals with type 2 diabetes in the CRONICAS cohort into Ahlqvist data-driven subtypes.
    \item To assess the transferability of a T2D polygenic risk score validated in a native mesoamerican sample to the Puno site of the CRONICAS cohort.
    \item To examine whether polygenic liability to T2D differs across subtypes in the Puno site of the CRONICAS cohort.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
